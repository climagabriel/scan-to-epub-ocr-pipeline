## The source

A. N. Kolmogorov, "Foundations of the Theory of Probability", Chelsea
Publishing Company, New York, 1950 — Nathan Morrison's English translation of
the 1933 German "Grundbegriffe der Wahrscheinlichkeitsrechnung". A measure-
theoretic monograph: axiomatic probability, Borel fields, Lebesgue integration,
conditional probability and expectation, laws of large numbers.

The OCR is tesseract 5 --psm 6 on a 400 dpi bilevel scan. Prose comes through
almost perfectly. MATH DOES NOT. These are the failure modes you will see, and
they are the entire reason this job exists:

- Subscripts and superscripts are flattened into commas and stray letters.
  "x_1, x_2, ..., x_n" arrives as "x,, %,..., én" or "Dit pet... + pat...-=1".
- Whole display equations are DROPPED, leaving a gap between two prose lines.
- Greek letters become Latin lookalikes: xi -> "é" or "&", mu -> "u",
  sigma -> "o", epsilon -> "e".
- Script/fraktur set symbols (the book uses fraktur B for the Borel field and
  fraktur F for a field of events) become "B", "$", "%", "R", "BY", "BF".
- Summation and integral signs become ">", "S", "J", "f".
- Primes, overbars and hats vanish.

## Your task

For each page you are given, emit a LaTeX body fragment.

## Rules

1. PROCESS EXACTLY ONE PAGE AT A TIME AND EMIT ITS RESULT IMMEDIATELY, before
   you read or think about the next page. Do NOT read all pages first. Do NOT
   accumulate results and emit them at the end. If you run out of budget
   mid-run, everything already emitted must still be usable.

2. Wrap each page's output in these exact delimiters, on their own lines:
       <<<PAGE p-NN>>>
       ...latex body fragment...
       <<<END p-NN>>>
   Use the NN from the input page header. Emit nothing else between blocks.

3. Reconstruct mathematics into correct LaTeX. Use \[ \] for display math and
   $ $ inline. Use \mathfrak{B} for the Borel field and \mathfrak{F} for a
   field of events. Use \mathsf{P} for probability, \mathsf{E} for expectation.
   Standard: \sum, \int, \subset, \cap, \cup, \varnothing, \to, \infty.

4. THE RULE THAT MATTERS MOST. You cannot see the page image — only this
   mangled text. Where the OCR is too corrupt to reconstruct with confidence,
   or where a display equation has obviously been dropped and you would have to
   guess its content, DO NOT GUESS. Emit:
       \UNSURE{brief description of what is missing or doubtful}
   in place of the formula. A \UNSURE marker is a success: a human will read
   the page image and fill it in. An invented formula that looks plausible is
   the single worst outcome of this job, because it is undetectable downstream.
   When torn between reconstructing and marking, MARK.

5. Preserve structure. Use \chapter{}, \section{}, \subsection{} for the book's
   headings; the book numbers chapters with Roman numerals and sections with
   "§ n". Use \begin{theorem}, \begin{proof} environments where the text says
   THEOREM / Proof. Use \footnote{} for footnotes. Preserve numbered equations
   as \begin{equation} with the book's own number via \tag{}.

6. Drop running heads and page numbers — the line at the top of a page like
   "18 II. Infinite Probability Fields" or "70 Appendix" is a running head, not
   content. Do not emit it.

7. Fix obvious OCR damage in PROSE silently (e.g. "PROBABIBLITY" -> the correct
   word, "integra- tion" rejoined across a line break). Do not modernise the
   author's spelling, punctuation or wording otherwise. This is a 1950 text and
   reads like one; leave it alone.

8. Output ONLY the delimited LaTeX blocks. No preamble, no \documentclass, no
   commentary, no summary, no closing remarks.
