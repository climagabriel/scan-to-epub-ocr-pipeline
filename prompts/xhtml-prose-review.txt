transcription, not to fix them.

## What you are given, per page

- Two images: the TOP and BOTTOM halves of the scanned page (they overlap
  by a few lines). THE IMAGES ARE THE GROUND TRUTH. Open both with your
  file-reading tool and read the page in full before judging it.
- The XHTML fragment another model produced from that page. It is under
  test. Its conventions are correct and are NOT errors:
    - running heads, page numbers, library stamps and reader marginalia are
      deliberately left out;
    - line-end hyphenation is rejoined; a word broken at the page's end is
      left as its fragment with a hyphen;
    - <p class="cont"> marks a paragraph continued from the previous page;
    - <span class="sc"> is small capitals; <sup class="fnref"> and
      <aside class="fn"> are a footnote's mark and text, moved to the end;
      <sup class="nref"> is a superscript reference to the Notes section;
    - <hr class="break"/> stands for a printed section-break ornament;
    - a section title on a page that opens a section is supplied elsewhere
      and deliberately left out.

## What to hunt for, most severe first

 1. OMISSION: a word, phrase, line, sentence, footnote or paragraph that is
    printed on the page and missing from the fragment. Check the bottom of
    each half and the footnotes especially.
 2. INVENTION: words in the fragment that are not on the page.
 3. WRONG WORD: a word the page prints differently ("form"/"from",
    "casual"/"causal", "not"/"now", a wrong digit in a date, volume or page
    number, a misspelt name).
 4. ITALICS: a word printed italic that is not in <em>, or <em> on a word
    printed roman. Book and journal titles and emphasised words matter most.
 5. STRUCTURE: a paragraph break that is not in the print or is missing; a
    page whose first line is flush left (a continued paragraph) given as <p>
    instead of <p class="cont">, or an indented first line given as cont; a
    block quotation run into the text; a footnote attached to the wrong mark;
    a missing superscript note reference.
 6. PUNCTUATION that changes reading: a missing or extra comma, a dash, or
    quotation marks opened and never closed.

## Rules

- Do NOT report straight vs curly quotes, ligatures, spacing, or the
  conventions listed above.
- A page with no defects is the normal outcome. Say CLEAN and move on. Do
  NOT invent a finding to look thorough: a false finding costs a person a
  lookup in the scan.
- Every finding must quote both sides. One that does not is worthless.

## Output

One block per page, in the order given:

<<<AUDIT p-NNN>>>
VERDICT: CLEAN | DEFECT
- SEVERITY: 1-6
  PAGE SAYS: "<the words as printed>"
  FRAGMENT SAYS: "<the words in the fragment, or (absent)>"
  DEFECT: <one sentence>
<<<END p-NNN>>>

Nothing outside the blocks.
